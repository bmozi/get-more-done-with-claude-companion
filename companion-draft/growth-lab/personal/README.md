# Choose, practice and review one change

Read Chapters 1 and 39; use Chapter 13 for tutoring and 45 for a feasible week. Use only [starting-note.txt](starting-note.txt). Do not submit personal health records or coworkers' private information.

## Discover

Before opening Claude, write Nora's observation and her interpretation on separate lines. Then give Claude the starting note and the coaching request from Chapter 39. Answer as a person rehearsing this fictional case; do not invent Nora's history. If asked for an unknown fact, say it is unknown. Ask for one alternative explanation and what observation would help distinguish it.

Keep the first response. Is the assistant asking something you can answer? Is it inventing a motive, condition, or personality? A useful conversation should help you understand a choice, not assign your identity.

## Learn and rehearse

Ask for an explanation of claim, evidence and request. Write a twenty-second contribution, then ask Claude to play a colleague requesting clarification. Answer in your own words. A fictional role-play response is practice, not feedback from an actual colleague.

## Act and review

Choose a preparation action that fits Wednesday's twenty minutes. Record how you would inspect it and what remains to check. Use the following supplied variation instead of inventing a successful meeting: Thursday's meeting is canceled. Write what was completed, what was not, and an appropriate next choice. Keep the goal open to reconsideration.

## Transfer

Without the earlier answer, help a fictional reader prepare a short explanation of a household maintenance need. The reader has only a label and an unreadable receipt; no service date is established. What can they learn or prepare before making a claim about service history? Do not turn the coaching prompt into a demand for a confident answer.

Keep your first question, a revised question, one concept explained without copying, a rehearsal, an action note and a review. If you use a real low-stakes goal later, record your actual attempt separately from Nora's designed example.

## Try a personal interest
