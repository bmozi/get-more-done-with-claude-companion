## Personal growth: avoid filling in an unseen action

Claude began with one question about what Leah pictured doing in the first minute. After a supplied reply, it explained contour drawing and proposed practice she could choose and adjust. It did not generate a picture as a substitute for her practice.

When told that the free period disappeared and no drawing was made, it correctly retained the prepared materials but also asserted, “She hasn’t looked at the cup’s edge.” The input did not establish that. We asked it to repair the unsupported negative claim. It then recorded whether she looked as unknown and clarified that these were supplied fictional facts rather than an observation of a real person.

This distinction matters in coaching. An unfinished result does not tell us every action someone took or why they took it. Ask for the missing information. A helpful response can still need that correction.
