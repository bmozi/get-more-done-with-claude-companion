# Chapter 25 practice guide

Document a small business process

## Objective

Investigate a customer need, shape and test a small offer, and turn an operating routine into a usable procedure.

## Inputs

- [Connected business practice](../growth-lab/business/README.md)
- [shop-policy-and-messages.txt](../shared/shop-policy-and-messages.txt)

## Practice and assessment

Use Amara's supplied interview notes to draft an evidence-linked offer and interpret the unsuccessful trial. Reproduce the four-hour and six-hour calculations. Keep one next experiment and a result that would make you reconsider it. Then document a recurring task using the shop example below.

Draft a one-page intake-response procedure with a normal case, a missing-information case, and an exception requiring human review. Produce sample responses from the supplied fictional messages, then check them against the policy. Supply the policy and message text to the assistant, but omit the parenthetical reviewer hints after Cases A–D. Those hints explain the intended assessment; they are not customer messages.

**Change one condition:** The owner is absent for two days. Which responses can staff prepare or send, and which commitments must wait? Make that authority explicit rather than assuming the assistant can fill the gap.

**Keep:** The procedure, sample inputs, review criteria, and named escalation role. A small, well-defined workflow is a stronger starting point than an assistant advertised as able to run the business.

## Record the attempt

Record date, product, interface, model shown, relevant settings, input version, actual output, checks, corrections, and unresolved questions. Do not put account identifiers or authentication material in the record.
