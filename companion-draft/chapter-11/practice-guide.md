# Chapter 11 practice guide

Research a question worth answering

## Objective

Turn a broad topic into a decision question, investigate primary sources, and write a brief whose claims can be checked.

## Inputs

- [venue-estimates.csv](../shared/venue-estimates.csv)
- [event-brief.txt](../shared/event-brief.txt)

## Practice and assessment

Use `companion-draft/shared/venue-estimates.csv` together with `event-brief.txt` in that folder to write a one-page comparison. The brief matters: Oak is unavailable for the chosen session despite its lower quoted fee. A designed conclusion is, “Oak costs $15 less in the stated comparison, but cannot serve this session. Cedar remains the current choice at $150 before supplies and deposit; setup access and the other unresolved fields still need confirmation.” That is a qualified recommendation, not evidence of a reservation. Check every number independently. Then research one low-stakes real question using the same structure, preserving source dates and links.

**Change one condition:** A new authorized note confirms that Oak is now available for the session, replacing the old availability record, but requires a two-hour setup window your volunteers cannot cover. Price and availability now pass; staffing still fails. Explain why the lower fee does not make Oak usable yet. Identify the change needed, such as a different setup arrangement confirmed by the venue, rather than silently assuming volunteers will appear.

**Keep:** The brief and a claim register linking important statements to their evidence. A collection of links becomes research when it helps someone make a better-supported decision.
