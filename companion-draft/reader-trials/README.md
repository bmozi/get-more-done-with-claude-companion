# Reader trials: can someone use the lesson without us?

Status: prepared protocol; no human participants recruited and no learning results collected. This is a practical editorial trial, not a controlled educational study. Participation is voluntary. Use fictional tasks; do not collect personal account details or real client records. This protocol does not authorize contacting anyone.

## Give the learner only the materials they would actually receive

Provide the selected chapter, its linked companion inputs and ordinary setup instructions. Keep editorial explanations and reviewer answer notes separate. Record the source commit, chapter version, available device/software and relevant prior experience using the learner's own description. Assign an arbitrary participant code if keeping records. Ask before recording audio or video; written observations are enough.

Explain: “We are testing the instructions. If a step is unclear, tell us. You can stop at any time.” Never imply that difficulty proves the learner is incapable.

## Try three different types of task

Choose one route suited to the person's existing skills. These are editorial task choices, not claims of standardized assessment.

| Route | Supplied work | Observable result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Beginner | Chapter1, then its no-Tuesday variation | Learner creates a feasible schedule, checks task order and deadlines, and identifies the later infeasible change without inventing time. |
| Everyday evidence | Chapter6 supplied packet, then changed occupancy | Learner separates contradicted claims from unresolved ones, includes mandatory costs, and explains what evidence would settle the booking question. |
| Builder | Chapter47 plus recovery README and fictional source | Learner runs interruption, inspects the ledger, recovers and explains why lost acknowledgement does not imply no report. Terminal/Python experience is a prerequisite, not a hidden test. |

Do not combine a first terminal lesson with a test of advanced recovery unless the instructions explicitly teach both. If setup blocks progress, record the setup defect separately from the reasoning task.

## Observe before helping

Ask the reader to state what they think the next step means, attempt it, inspect the result and explain their decision. Do not supply the missing step immediately. If they request help, provide it, but record the exact intervention. Assisted completion remains useful evidence; do not relabel it independent completion.

Record the first output, the observed difficulty, the chapter passage involved, any help given and the final result. Avoid interpreting silence as understanding or a positive reaction as learning. Ask which instruction was least clear and what they would change.

## Check transfer

Hide the worked answer and offer the chapter's changed condition. Ask the reader to explain why the original answer must change. If they only reproduce the original numbers, return to the reasoning. With their agreement, ask for a different harmless example later; record the interval rather than assuming immediate success persists.

Success for this small editorial trial means the person can complete the specified task, detect the important changed condition, explain their check and name an unresolved limit without an unrecorded rescue. One person's success does not establish effectiveness for all beginners, accessibility across devices or a causal learning advantage over another book.

## Turn observations into repairs

Label the finding: observed defect, structural weakness, local wording issue, deliberate tradeoff or untested preference. Link it to the task and passage. Repair the material, rebuild the reader copy, and invite a new attempt only if the person wishes. Preserve the initial failure. Keep results anonymous in public material unless there is explicit permission for attribution.

The included record is blank on purpose. A prepared protocol closes a preparation gap; only an actual attempt can supply human evidence.
